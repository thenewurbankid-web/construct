## Notes (written after the run, from r2.jsonl rows and r2-raw/)

**H (blocks, no driver) compared with F (7b code-shaped brief, splice) and E (7b brief, best-of-5 whole file):** H
8/12, E 8/12, F 6/12. H passes t07 and t10, which E and F both fail on tsc. Those two failures are wiring: imports
the coder used but never wrote. H's prelude adds the imports deterministically (r2.jsonl:43, :46 for F;
r2.jsonl:31, :34 for E). E and F pass t04 and t08, which H fails. H needs sampling: pass is 1/11 at N=1, 6/11 at
N=3 and 7/11 at N=5. E does not: every one of E's passes came from its first sample, so its N=3/5 samples added
nothing. H uses no 7b tokens; its wall time (81 s) is lower than E's (111 s) and F's (107 s).

**What H lacks, per failing task.** These are the parts where the driver really needs a model, or a better block.
- t04 (r2.jsonl:76): the SPEC intent is that the not-found branch and the found branch share one "Back to the
  catalogue" link. The format template cannot derive that. Samples were check, or a fragment that re-declared
  `Book` next to the function.
- t05 (r2.jsonl:77): 1.5b echoes the brief's TYPES IN SCOPE block back. The signature-only view (`{ ... }`
  bodies) gets pasted into the reply, and spliceApplier rejects it as fragment in 4 of 5 samples. A prose brief
  from the 7b does not invite that echo. This is a briefBuilder presentation problem, not missing knowledge.
- t08 (r2.jsonl:80): the list template's worked example has a remove Button, and the empty-state task copies it.
  Button is not in this task's context, so the prelude never imports it (tsc). Choosing which part of a
  template applies to this task is judgement that the static template lacks. (r3's importFixer covers the
  missing import.)
- t12 (r2.jsonl:84): the select renders, but the chosen genre never filters the list (check: History still shows
  every book). The spec intent "picking a genre shows only that genre's books" is in the brief. The 1.5b did not
  connect the option value to the filter. Every 7b arm also fails t12 (B check, E/F tsc).

**I (qwen3:4b, native tools) compared with F and H:** I 2/12, F 6/12, H 8/12. When the 4b made a tool call it
called the right tool every time (right-tool rate 100%). But only 41% of its turns made a native call. The rest
were plain-text reasoning (`think: false` and `/no_think` did not stop it), so 10 of 12 tasks ran out of the 8
turns. It also always called `code` with n=1, which throws away the best-of-5 that carries H. Its cost was
373k/80k tokens and 1199 s, compared with H's 32k/11k and 81 s.

**F-sr (search/replace): 0/12, all format.** 1.5b ignored the SEARCH/REPLACE instruction and returned the whole
file every time (r2.jsonl:49-60, e.g. r2-raw/F-sr/t01-book-by-author/02-coder.md).

**G (FIM, instruct tag; no base tag is pulled): 2/12.** 6 of its 10 failures are the 7b skeleton missing the
/*FILL*/ marker (r2.jsonl:64, :66-68, :70, :72).
